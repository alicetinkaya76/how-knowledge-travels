# How Knowledge Travels: Islam in Different Cultures

Exploring the history of science through translation, learning and the development of new knowledge.

Dr. Ali Çetinkaya’s English presentation for *Feel the Islamic Spirit*, 22 November 2026.

[Open the presentation](https://alicetinkaya76.github.io/how-knowledge-travels/).

The revised 100-minute session follows the work people had to do before knowledge could become understandable and useful in another community. Six connected acts move from finding and translating books to new explanations, teaching, new readers and living Muslim cultures. There are **34 main slides**, including **5 minutes of audience questions at 95:00**, and **6 optional source slides** available through Contents.

The opening establishes the setting before the first map. A new context scene explains why Greek books mattered and introduces Syriac readers and learning from India and Iran. Real Greek and Arabic pages show the same short medical saying. The later Canon example separately follows an Arabic text into Gerard’s Latin tradition. Fibonacci now follows Chaucer, connecting two ways of teaching new readers. Plain English explains the problem, the evidence and why each example matters.

Use the arrow keys for slides and reveals. Press **F** for full screen, **B** to hide or restore controls, **M** for Contents and **C** for sources. Select archive images to enlarge them. The normal path stops at questions; optional scenes have a return button. Leaflet maps include dated places and individually playable connections. Translation controls match words across real texts; the astrolabe controls highlight parts in a real photograph with English and Arabic terms.

The single HTML file embeds images, fonts, demonstrations and the Natural Earth base map for offline use. Optional online street tiles require an internet connection. Captions identify historical objects, later copies, modern demonstrations and teaching reconstructions.
