HOW KNOWLEDGE TRAVELS — PURPOSE OF EACH SCENE

S01 · How Knowledge Travels: Islam in Different Cultures
Knowledge becomes useful when someone can understand and use it; moving a book or tool is only the beginning.

S02 · You already speak this history
Familiar words preserve clues to earlier learning, but we must examine the methods as well as their names.

S03 · Travel, and look
Looking closely helps us move from admiring an object to understanding the work behind it.

S04 · Knowledge is a trust
Caring for knowledge includes checking it and helping another person understand it.

S05 · Why look for Greek books?
Older books were sought because they offered explanations people could study, question and use; language skills opened that learning to new readers.

S06 · A medical book finds Arabic readers
Translation gave an Arabic reader access to medical explanations that had previously required another language.

S07 · Ḥunayn searches for a missing book
Finding a book meant finding usable pages; without them, even a skilled translator could not recover the whole argument.

S08 · Ḥunayn returns to his own translation
Comparing sources before correcting a translation gave later readers a clearer text to study and question.

S09 · One thought, two languages
Translation makes a thought available to another reader by finding words that point to the right meaning, not just matching letters.

S10 · A translation needs a book
A usable copy depended on materials and skilled work; finding, borrowing, buying and commissioning were different kinds of access.

S11 · The bookseller’s list
A bookseller’s knowledge connected the search for texts with the trade and materials needed to make copies.

S12 · Paper travelled through a network
A steady supply of writing materials helped turn one text into books that more people could study.

S13 · Algebra: from a rule to a reason
The learner can explain the calculation and find an error, rather than remember a rule without understanding it.

S14 · How can light turn a scene upside down?
A small opening makes the paths of light visible, so we can explain an upside-down image and predict what will change.

S15 · Three lamps, one opening
Covering one lamp lets us test a specific claim; covering the shared opening would hide the very difference we need to study.

S16 · A new answer inside the eye
Books carried questions and diagrams that later readers could examine and change, rather than a final answer that nobody questioned.

S17 · A clock that starts again
Another maker needs the hidden reset explained, so the skill can survive beyond the original machine and its maker.

S18 · Who pays? Who teaches?
Learning continued because people paid for it and teachers checked what their students were allowed to pass on.

S19 · Who funds a place to learn?
A continuing income could keep teaching available across generations; similar needs do not by themselves prove a direct institutional origin.

S20 · How the Canon became a textbook
The Canon connected principles, illness and treatment in a system that Latin-reading teachers could use to organize medical education.

S21 · What could a student learn from the Canon?
A learner connects an action with the injury and checks the conditions of a sign before deciding what it means.

S22 · When a translator checks the numbers
One unchecked table entry can affect many later answers; repeating the calculation can stop the error being passed on.

S23 · The sky in your hand
The instrument can travel, but its user must adapt the local horizon rather than assume one setting works everywhere.

S24 · Take an astrolabe apart
Separating the parts shows how a local setting can change while the carefully made star map remains useful.

S25 · One instrument, new readers
A translated label can help the next user, while an unchecked numerical correction can make the same object less reliable.

S26 · Chaucer teaches the astrolabe in English
The learner's language, starting knowledge and next practical step shape a lesson that can actually be used.

S27 · From Béjaïa to a merchant’s classroom
A boy's education in North Africa helped a written method reach new readers; practical lessons made it useful in everyday trade.

S28 · Paper travels in both directions
A Qur'an on Italian paper shows Muslim communities remaining active makers and users in an exchange that moved in several directions.

S29 · Timbuktu’s living archive
The books preserve what African Muslim readers studied; protecting them keeps that work available to others.

S30 · One prayer, two buildings
Shared Muslim worship can use different local materials and skills; caring for the buildings teaches those skills again.

S31 · Jawi and Ottoman Turkish
Adapting letters let people write their own histories and practical knowledge while keeping a shared connection with Arabic.

S32 · What made these journeys possible?
Knowledge travelled because people made it understandable and useful; Muslim communities gave that learning different local forms.

S33 · Rabbi zidnī ʿilmā
